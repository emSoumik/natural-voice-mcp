## part 1: soumik's own writing (level 2)

### sample A: bio

> i'm a **cs undergrad**. mostly into **product design** and **frontend**, and increasingly building around **ai**. still figuring out the exact label, but somewhere between **designer and engineer**.

**why it works**
- three short sentences, then one longer one with a turn ("still figuring out... but...").
- subject drop: "mostly into", "still figuring out".
- honest about the label instead of claiming a title.
- bold on anchors a skimmer should catch.

### sample B: community / program application

> i've been following ai plus friends since their first dubai showcase, watching how they build and present real ai agents in live settings made me want to do the same thing in my own university. put together a demo of my own and then went a step further. i organized and delivered a lecture and workshop on ai agents and the future of ai for my juniors. most of the students i taught had only ever interacted with basic **chatbots like chatgpt**. they had no idea what ai agents actually are or how they work. my goal was to break that barrier and show them how agents can **optimize real workflows and everyday tasks**, not just answer simple questions.
>
> it felt great to be the one teaching that to people just one or two years behind me. i want to keep doing more of this because i genuinely believe understanding ai agents early on will be a huge advantage for anyone entering tech.

**why it works**
- story arc: saw it → did it → went further → who it was for → what they learned → what's next.
- loose comma splice in the first sentence reads spoken. Keep that kind of thing.
- specific audience detail ("had only ever interacted with basic chatbots").
- one feeling, stated once ("it felt great").
- one "not just" trailing clause, one "genuinely". Both at budget.
- ends on intent plus a reason.

### sample C: course / scholarship SOP

> my goal is to build a career in ui/ux design engineering. i've spent a lot of time exploring this field and i genuinely believe it's where my strengths and passions align. this google ux design course is exactly the kind of structured learning i need to build real skills and break into the industry. it covers fundamentals like user research, wireframing, prototyping, and usability testing, all of which are essential for the career path i'm pursuing.
>
> in the short term, my goal is to complete this course and build a solid portfolio with at least three to four projects that showcase my design thinking and problem solving abilities. i want to get comfortable with tools like figma and develop a strong foundation in design principles that i can build on.
>
> in the long term, i aim to land an internship or entry level role in ux design where i can learn from experienced designers and contribute to real products. eventually, i want to work at a company where i can design digital experiences that genuinely help people and make their lives easier.

**why it works:** goal ladder structure, plain goal statements, concrete short term target (three to four projects).

**what a polish pass would tighten** (the voice is right, a few AI-ish words slipped in):
- "genuinely" appears twice. Keep one.
- "strengths and passions align", "showcase", "essential for the career path i'm pursuing", "digital experiences" drift toward application-speak.

**tightened version**

> my goal is to build a career in ui/ux design engineering. i've spent a lot of time exploring the field and it's where what i'm good at and what i enjoy overlap. this google ux design course is the structured learning i need to build real skills and break in. it covers user research, wireframing, prototyping, and usability testing, which is most of what the job actually is.
>
> in the short term, i want to finish the course and build a portfolio with three to four projects that show how i think through design problems. i also want to get comfortable in figma and build a strong base in design principles.
>
> in the long term, i want an internship or entry level role in ux where i can learn from experienced designers and work on real products. eventually, i want to design things that genuinely make people's lives easier.

## part 2: broadcast reference (level 3)

The user likes the style of certain personal newsletter emails from community builders. Patterns observed, not text to copy:

- opens with "hi." or "hi [group], [name] here!"
- one thought per line, lots of white space
- a self intro that admits a limit up front ("i'm not an engineer." / "i still dont know if this is a company.")
- credibility through concrete specifics: numbers, cities, who built what in how long
- example list of real people's projects ("a 15 yo kid built...", "a mother built...")
- `tldr:` with 3 dashed items for longer emails
- section labels as plain lowercase lines ("new free stuff for you.")
- exact logistics: date, time, place, link, deadline with timezone
- warm casual markers: dw, pls, ty, btw, haha, <3, :-)
- signs off with just the name
